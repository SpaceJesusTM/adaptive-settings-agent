# Design Audit Against HCI Literature (Comprehensive UX & Settings Pass)

This audit reviews every major architectural and UX decision introduced in `feat/comprehensive-ux-settings-pass` against seven peer-reviewed HCI papers on adaptive interfaces, mixed-initiative customization, interaction-data transparency, and explanation demand. **No human-participant study has been conducted**; literature citations justify design hypotheses and expose uncomfortable trade-offs, while automated tests verify technical invariants only.

## 2. Literature Grounding Summary (Verified Sources)

1. **Findlater, L., & McGrenere, J. (2004).** *A Comparison of Static, Adaptive, and Adaptable Menus.* CHI 2004. `https://doi.org/10.1145/985692.985704`
   - Empirical split-menu study (n=27) comparing static, adaptive, and adaptable designs; adaptable user-controlled customization outperformed automatic adaptive rearrangement on efficiency/preference, informing our decision to keep canonical menus spatially invariant and isolate AI suggestions inside the Adaptive Workspace.
2. **Findlater, L., & McGrenere, J. (2010).** *Beyond Performance: Feature Awareness in Personalized Interfaces.* International Journal of Human-Computer Studies (IJHCS), 68(3). `https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581909001566`
   - Three controlled studies showing personalization efficiency gains can trade off against awareness of non-promoted settings. Motivated keeping every canonical setting in its authentic menu location, adding category coverage auditing in `planner.py`, and providing `Show in settings` cross-links.
3. **Bunt, A., Conati, C., & McGrenere, J. (2007).** *Supporting Interface Customization Using a Mixed-Initiative Approach.* IUI 2007.
   - MICA combined predictive assistance and explicit customization rationale while preserving final user control. Directly shapes our explainable rationale vs canonical-description split and user-veto workflow (`Changes`).
4. **Todi, K., Bailly, G., Leiva, L., & Oulasvirta, A. (2021).** *Adapting User Interfaces with Model-based Reinforcement Learning.* CHI 2021. `https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445497`
   - Combined predictive HCI models with Monte Carlo Tree Search so adaptations weigh expected utility against transition/relearning cost. Motivates zero-forced-change restraint (`well_configured_competitive` scenario) and the `Discover` card-hold rule.
5. **Kim, T. S., Chowdhury, S., Song, K., & Suh, B. (2026).** *In-Situ Adaptive Interfaces for Online Browsing: Design Dimensions for Intent-Responsive Automation and User Control.* IUI 2026. `https://doi.org/10.1145/3742413.3789092`
   - Taxonomizes automation degree, initiation timing, and explicit user control/reversibility for intent-responsive interfaces. Grounds our scope classifier (`explain_only` / `focused` / `category_optimization` / `broad_optimization` / `full_game_setup`), auto-staging without auto-apply, and server-side atomic undo/lock enforcement.
6. **Alves, T., Duarte, C., Montague, K., & Guerreiro, T. (2026).** *Exploring the Role of Interaction Data to Empower End-User Decision-Making in UI Personalization.* CHI 2026. `https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791022`
   - Vignette interviews (n=12) demonstrating that surfacing structured interaction rationale empowers reflective personalization, while uncurated telemetry risks overload. Directly informs hiding `Trace` behind the Experimenter toggle and replacing inline telemetry clutter with collapsible `What I checked` and provenance pills.
7. **Ingesson, O., & Riveiro, M. (2026).** *When Do Users (Not) Want Explanations?* DIS 2026. `https://doi.org/10.1145/3800645.3812974` *(Note: verified author spelling is Riveiro.)*
   - Mixed-methods study (n=280) showing explanation demand varies selectively with task difficulty, confidence, and time/incentive pressure, and that merely exposing explanations does not automatically improve user reliance or accuracy. Reinforces both our progressive explanation disclosure in the UI and our research requirement to evaluate **independent setting comprehension** in [PARTICIPANT_PILOT.md](PARTICIPANT_PILOT.md) rather than assuming explanations equal understanding.

## 3. Open Empirical Questions (Requires Human Study)

- **Auto-staging vs manual staging error rate:** Does eliminating the intermediate "Stage all" click reduce tedium on comprehensive setups without increasing accidental approval of unwanted recommendations?
- **Review scalability across large plans:** At what staged item count (e.g., 15–25 changes across Video/Audio/Game) do participants switch from per-item inspection to rubber-stamping "Apply selected changes"?
- **Cross-game mental model transfer:** Do explicit abstract dimensions ("clarity", "input latency") and provenance badges help CS2 newcomers transfer preferences from VALORANT accurately without assuming numeric sensitivity parity?
