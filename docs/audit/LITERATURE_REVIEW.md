# GamePlan literature evidence and experimental traceability

This review uses the UX baseline at `c75d81e2e726fd14e26a3f31e6961f925cb097f4`. All nine primary full texts were available. Section references identify the passages examined. Each **GamePlan experiment** is an inference from the evidence and a proposed test in the settings domain. See [Evaluation results](EVALUATION_RESULTS.md) for the experiments that actually ran. Catalogue and API tests can establish simulator behavior. Feature awareness, learning, and perceived control require participant measures.

## Shared preferences and immediate objectives

### MAESTRO

**Evidence.** Lee, Abbas, Lee, Kim, and Chen, UIST 2026. Examined arXiv v2 §§3.1–3.6, 4–6, and Appendix A, including A.6. A GPT-5.4 extractor runs on each utterance and maintains shared session-scoped records with a natural-language description, hard/soft strength, and relevant workflow stages. Explicit constraints and desires enter memory, an ordinary click does not, and ambiguous strength defaults to soft. The GUI augments options with supporting metadata while the workflow agent uses the same records to assess feasibility and suggest backtracking. Dead ends are scoped to the failed path and invalidated if their underlying preference changes. In a within-subject study with 33 people, MAESTRO reduced unmet hard preferences in final bookings (`p=.047`) and hard-preference violations during selection (`p=.008`). Binary task success and completion time differences were not statistically significant. The condition combined a different layout, adaptation, and navigation in a linear movie-booking task with assigned preferences. The study did not isolate extraction accuracy or evaluate cross-session memory. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830398.3830672) · [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06134v2) · [Author project](https://sangwooklee.info/publications/maestro/)

**GamePlan experiment.** `ProfileService.dimension_weights` sums session goals, preferences, and events, while `classify_scope`, the coverage planner, and Discover's `rank_candidates` each interpret related inputs. Test a shared, game-aware effective state that gives explicit exclusions authority over ranking and distinguishes current goals, durable preferences, and observed facts. Preserve canonical menu visibility and carry the evidence into suggestion cards. Measure protected-setting violations, correct scope, recurrence after correction, goal score, history fidelity, and local-model call count on mixed requests and cross-game histories. This tests DG1–DG4 without adopting booking-stage backtracking or hiding settings.

**Artifacts.** Appendix A.6 publishes the extractor, planner, and baseline system prompts starting on PDF page 16. Appendix A contains example tasks, interactions, and adapted interfaces. The MIT-licensed movie implementation exposes matching runtime prompt and memory files at inspected commit `b8d132c17c94e5e59ab859bf2dc7b0291c1b31fa`. [Extractor](https://github.com/wooogler/maestro-movie/blob/b8d132c17c94e5e59ab859bf2dc7b0291c1b31fa/apps/tuning-agent/src/llm/llmExtractor.ts) · [Planner](https://github.com/wooogler/maestro-movie/blob/b8d132c17c94e5e59ab859bf2dc7b0291c1b31fa/apps/tuning-agent/src/llm/llmPlanner.ts) · [Memory](https://github.com/wooogler/maestro-movie/blob/b8d132c17c94e5e59ab859bf2dc7b0291c1b31fa/apps/tuning-agent/src/core/cpMemory.ts) · [License](https://github.com/wooogler/maestro-movie/blob/b8d132c17c94e5e59ab859bf2dc7b0291c1b31fa/LICENSE)

### Just-In-Time Objectives

**Evidence.** Lam, Shaikh, Xu, Guo, Yang, Heer, Landay, and Bernstein, CHI 2026. Examined §§3–6 and Appendix A. An upstream model induces a brief name, a one- or two-sentence objective description, and an importance weight, then reuses the objective for generation and evaluation. Poppins exposes intermediate objectives, expertise, and tool designs for selection or editing. In two screenshot studies (`N=14` and `N=205`), 75.0% and 76.6% of induced objectives received one of the top two accuracy ratings. Generated feedback won 71.4% and 66.1% of head-to-head comparisons against an unsteered baseline. A separate 17-person writing study found practical friction in steering deep intermediate controls. Participants selected their own screenshots and some objectives missed intent at the needed level of granularity. The system used hosted models and examined best-of-N generation, with a different latency envelope from local GamePlan. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790713) · [Full text §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14591#S3)

**GamePlan experiment.** `ProfileService.session_goals` preserves objective text, while `dimension_weights` reduces its effect to fixed dimensions and `scope.classify_scope` independently interprets the current turn. Reuse a short scoped objective and explicit protected settings across candidate selection, recommendation context, validation, and Discover. Use typed chat and simulator state as input. Compare one compact shared representation to the current dimension-only ranking and a size-matched single prompt. Measure correction, goal-switch relevance, restore-of-priority behavior, latency, and unnecessary clarifications. An explicit instruction should outrank an inferred objective. This tests DG2–DG4.

**Artifacts.** Published Appendix A gives exact objective induction, expert and tool generation, evaluation, UI generation, and critique templates. Poppins source and the study's example prompts and outputs are linked from the paper. [Prompts, PDF page 22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.14591#page=22) · [Poppins source](https://github.com/michelle123lam/poppins)

## Discover and inspectable evidence

### ReLay

**Evidence.** Kim, Chowdhury, Song, and Suh, IUI 2026. Examined §§3–7, Appendix A, and the controls in Appendix B. A browser probe used scroll, click, dwell, and visible elements to classify broad exploration versus focused comparison at two-minute intervals. Intent persisted within a browsing domain until contrary evidence and restarted on domain change. The probe adjusted information hierarchy, card detail, and the ordering of previously seen items. Users could undo, toggle, or save domain-scoped overrides. Across a two-phase study with 10 people, 50 of 362 logged adaptations were overridden and 28 of those overrides occurred in the first five minutes. Participants described testing what the system would do and valued immediate reversible controls. No static control condition isolates a causal workload or efficiency effect. Study sites used preconfigured structural parsing, so interaction responsiveness does not establish the production costs of dynamic DOM processing. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3742413.3789092) · [Full text](https://gracekim.me/docs/AdaptiveInterfaces.pdf)

**GamePlan experiment.** `RelevantNowService._collect_candidates` interleaves fixed trigger groups and `get` maintains a snapshot with statuses. Define meaningful refresh events, preserve cards across incidental interactions, and keep dismissal and recovery close to the surface. Compare current snapshots against a deterministic eligibility and abstention policy. Replay fresh game entry, repeated GETs, ordinary navigation, setting edits, explicit changes of objective, and Undo. Count ID and value churn, dismissed cards returning, and justified refreshes. Handle dismissal as feedback on the offered suggestion and reserve preference changes for distinct evidence. Test DG1, DG3, and DG4.

**Artifacts.** Appendix A reproduces representative DOM-analysis, granularity, and intent-detection prompts. Appendices B and C show controls and report additional measures. No public ReLay source repository or raw interaction dataset was verified from the paper and project searches. [Prompt appendix, PDF page 20](https://gracekim.me/docs/AdaptiveInterfaces.pdf#page=20)

### Reflexive personalization from interaction data

**Evidence.** Alves, Duarte, Montague, and Guerreiro, CHI 2026. Examined §§3–5 and the vignette and supplement descriptions. Twelve people discussed 42 design probes spanning data-only dashboards, textual suggestions, visual before/after suggestions, and visual suggestions with social information. Participants could identify opportunities through interaction data and favored visual suggestions as starting points, with evidence useful for evaluating doubtful suggestions and deciding the effort was worthwhile. Interviews examined interpretation and preference using synthetic data and hypothetical interfaces, so the study cannot estimate behavioral adoption or measured efficiency gains. [Proceedings and supplements](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791022) · [Full text](https://techandpeople.github.io/downloads/InteractionDataToEmpowerDecisionMaking.pdf) · [Study materials](https://osf.io/8awdj/?view_only=9451ed14653b4a96857750ff9eeb9b3b)

**GamePlan experiment.** `observe_setting` identifies history sources and `build_observed_summary` supplies evidence to System Understanding. Applying an AI suggestion and editing manually are both deliberate. The action source determines whether the resulting value provides direct evidence of the player’s own choice or records acceptance of a suggestion. Show the actual prior selection, action source, game, value, and target comparison at the point of a suggestion, with a way to correct or dismiss the inference. Contrast an evidence-only view with a brief evidence-linked action. In simulator traces, test source attribution, correction, and repeat suggestions after correction. In a human study, ask players which evidence supported the card and what they could correct. This tests DG2–DG4. The study used vignettes and interview questions, so runtime LLM prompts and runnable UIPulse code are inapplicable.

### Feature awareness in personalized interfaces

**Evidence.** Findlater and McGrenere, IJHCS 68(3), 2010, pp. 121–137. Examined §§2–8, including the recognition-test design, Study 3 procedure and results, and limits of the awareness measures. Three controlled studies examined layered interfaces and adaptive split menus. The earlier recognition studies found the potential for better core-task performance alongside lower awareness of unused functionality. In Study 3 with 30 people, high-accuracy adaptive menus were fastest on previously used items, while the static control was faster on new items. The Study 3 recognition test had no significant difference. The effects concern interfaces that changed visibility or prominence. The authors identify suggestions of unused features as an opportunity that their designs did not establish. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2009.10.002) · [Full text](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/edapt/papers/findlater2010.pdf)

**GamePlan experiment.** Canonical settings remain available and recommendations link into them, but `rank_candidates` requires proposability and a useful alternate value, so this path misses relevant unfamiliar controls that are already configured well or are manual-only. Compare change-only suggestions with a bounded educational card for a known goal-relevant unfamiliar control. Evaluate useful unfamiliar-setting coverage without displacing high-value actions. In a human study, measure recognition and unaided location for a subsequent task alongside core task time. A card impression is evidence of surfacing a setting and requires a separate measure to establish awareness. This tests DG1 and DG2.

**Artifacts.** The paper includes the menu designs, task construction, and recognition protocol. No public code or dataset was verified on the lab publication page. This is a non-LLM study. [Lab publications](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/edapt/publications.html)

### Model-based adaptive interfaces

**Evidence.** Todi, Bailly, Leiva, and Oulasvirta, CHI 2021. Examined §§3–7, including menu action space, predictive cost, simulation, empirical evaluation, and future work. The MCTS policy simulated sequences of menu changes and included doing nothing among feasible actions. The predictive model estimated selection effort based on exposure, learned locations, and the cost of surprise. In a lab study with 18 participants, mean selection time was 2,162 ms for MCTS, 2,283 ms for static menus, and 2,298 ms for frequency reordering. Both comparisons to MCTS were significant under the reported post-hoc test. These were short 15-item menus with known distributions. The policy needs sufficiently accurate models of future user behavior and becomes harder to scale with more items. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3411764.3445497) · [Full text](https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/adaptive/resources/chi2021-todi-adaptive.pdf)

**GamePlan experiment.** `rank_candidates` admits candidates with a positive score after its feasibility and suppression checks and `_collect_candidates` merges by bucket priority. Add a conservative deterministic threshold and treat leaving the Discover surface quiet as a legitimate result. Force real feasibility, suppression, and redundancy checks before weighing relevant evidence and novelty. Report appropriate abstention, top-k precision and useful coverage together so an always-empty or always-full policy cannot look good. Compare end-to-end call count and adaptation latency. The evidence motivates accounting for costs. Calibrating the value of an interruption for a player remains untested. This addresses DG1 and DG4.

**Artifacts.** The authors publish adaptive-menu application code. Its README reserves all rights, so this review treats the repo as an inspection artifact. The method uses no LLM prompts. [Project and implementation](https://github.com/aalto-ui/chi21adaptive)

## Independent understanding and in-context correction

### Contrastive explanations

**Evidence.** Buçinca, Swaroop, Paluch, Doshi-Velez, and Gajos, CHI 2025. Examined §§3–8 and Appendix A.3. An expert-aligned model identified an exercise, a human-aligned model suggested a plausible foil, and an intermediate representation selected the differences to explain. GPT-4 narrated the supplied differences and outputs were manually checked. In a randomized experiment with 628 participants, pre-decision explanations with a predicted foil improved immediate unaided task performance relative to unilateral rationales while maintaining assisted accuracy. They also left overreliance on incorrect AI suggestions and worked unevenly across participants. The exercise task used vignettes and simulated AI errors, and no long-term retention was tested. The authors publish exact unilateral and contrastive templates in Appendix A.3. No separate runnable source was verified. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713229) · [Accepted full text](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/bucinca2025contrastive.pdf) · [Prompt appendix, PDF page 22](https://kgajos.seas.harvard.edu/papers/bucinca2025contrastive.pdf#page=22)

**GamePlan experiment.** `RecommendationCard` exposes what a setting does, why it was suggested, and trade-offs. `render_context` supplies compact catalogue records for explanations. Add optional, catalogue-grounded comparisons when the user names a plausible alternative or when a pair addresses an established distinction, such as ambient occlusion and cast shadows. Choose and verify the foil from explicit context or curated facts. Score correct distinctions, relevant trade-offs, unsupported claims, context fit, and proposal count for explanation-only turns. Test unaided transfer to a different configuration in a human study before claiming learning. This targets DG2 and DG3.

### Cocoa

**Evidence.** Feng, Pu, Latzke, August, Siangliulue, Bragg, Weld, Zhang, and Chang, CHI 2026. Examined arXiv v4 §§3–9 and Appendix D, then checked prompt locations in v3 Appendix I. Interactive plans let people edit, add, remove, and reassign steps. They can inspect an intermediate output, modify it, and repair downstream work. The within-subject lab comparison used the same GPT-4o research tools in both conditions. Sixteen researchers reported better steerability (median 4 vs. 3, `p=.005`) without a significant difference in ease of use or general utility. Seven researchers subsequently used it on their own work for a week. Participants and tasks were research-specific, and the comparison did not isolate individual affordances. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791673) · [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10999#S4)

**GamePlan experiment.** GamePlan already stages editable proposals in Changes and commits through `ProposalService.apply`. Test whether scope and dependency context can survive user edits and re-planning, with stale targets invalidated precisely. Use a player correction to preserve shadows while retaining unrelated staged choices. Compare time or turns to correct, unintended changes, staged-versus-committed provenance, and whether the player can undo just the intended historical change. This supports the existing explicit Apply boundary and tests DG2–DG4.

**Artifacts.** The v4 paper points to supplementary prompts and a user manual. v3 Appendix I directly publishes prompt templates for plan generation, converting a step to an instruction, and reformatting output. Those are versioned exemplars and exact identity with the CHI deployment was not verified. No standalone public Cocoa source was verified. [Versioned prompt appendix, PDF page 24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10999v3#page=24)

### DuetUI

**Evidence.** Xu, Xiang, Song, Sun, and Tong, CHI 2026. Examined §§3–9 and user-study Appendix B. A shared context manager connects task and interface state through a bidirectional action history. Triggered reasoning interprets meaningful direct manipulations where simple visual updates need no semantic processing. Its ten-task technical study compared direct prompting, a six-stage no-loop pipeline, and the full loop. The full loop had higher aggregate weighted F1 (0.508 vs. 0.459 for no loop) and better recall, with lower precision for some element types. Expert quality did not significantly differ between the two DuetUI variants. The 24-person study compared DuetUI with Stitch and found less time spent prompting and better usability ratings. Baseline and loop comparisons answer different questions. Simulated external services, a small campus sample, and cloud-model architecture limit applicability. [Proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790441) · [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13444#S5) · [Project and video](https://arkxlab.github.io/projects_collection/duet-ui/index.html)

**GamePlan experiment.** `Store.manual_change` records direct edits, `observe_setting` attributes the resulting values, and `prompts._state_lines` includes a compact observed summary in chat. Discover builds cards through a different candidate and snapshot pipeline. Feed both surfaces a compact projection of current settings changes and per-event provenance. Test a real A1 manual change followed by a B1 recommendation against untouched defaults, an AI-applied change, and an Undo. Measure agreement across surfaces, previously selected value accuracy, durable false positives, and irrelevance as the history grows. Preserve the existing widgets and stable navigation. This addresses DG1, DG3, and DG4.

**Artifacts.** Paper §5 describes the context and schema managers and refers to attached specifications. Appendix B provides instruments and task list. The public project page links the manuscript and demo. Exact runtime prompts, runnable source, and raw user traces were not verified from those sources.

## Technical evaluation precedents

**PrefEval.** Zhao, Hong, Liu, Hazarika, and Lin, ICLR 2025. Examined §§2–3, 6, and the data construction and prompt appendix. Preference/query items vary disclosure style (explicit, implicit choice, and implicit persona dialogue) and insert distractors before the later request. They score generation and constrained classification separately. The authors report steep zero-shot following degradation at about ten turns, which motivates supplying the relevant remembered record explicitly. Many tasks derive from LLM-assisted curation, and the benchmark is designed around a relevant preference. GamePlan needs paired negative controls where the memory is irrelevant, ambiguous, corrected, or contradicted by the current objective. Evaluate extraction and later following separately, check committed state deterministically, and keep open explanations under a factual rubric. Public data, scripts, and prompt templates are indexed by the project. [Full text and appendices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09597) · [Code and data](https://github.com/amazon-science/PrefEval)

**Small Models, Big Results.** Cohen, Halpern, Kahlon, Oren, Berkovitch, Caduri, Dagan, and Efros, EMNLP 2025. Examined §§3–8, limitations, and the prompt appendices. A first stage summarizes individual screenshots and actions and a tuned small model extracts intent from the combined summaries. Decomposition improved reported semantic-equivalence scores on UI-automation-derived trajectories, including noisy datasets. The training and evaluation tasks are retrospective intent extraction and provide no direct evidence for a zero-shot Gemma parser on mixed game-settings requests. GamePlan can use known IDs, explicit action sources, and compact structured state in lieu of repeatedly summarizing raw interaction history. Benchmark deterministic, single structured-model-pass, and multi-call candidates on the same settings inputs, with omitted intentions, false hard constraints, and latency scored separately. The paper publishes baseline and component prompt details in its appendices and identifies the source benchmark datasets. [ACL paper and attachments](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.949/)

## Decision rules for GamePlan

| Hypothesis | Smallest meaningful comparison | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| A shared effective objective prevents stale priorities and protects player constraints | Baseline vs. explicit immediate objective plus scoped preferences and independently checked exclusions | Fewer protected changes, fewer wrong-scope preferences, correct correction/clear behavior, and consistent chat/Discover attribution |
| Discover can be opportunistic and quiet | Baseline vs. deterministic evidence, suppression, and abstention policy | Appropriate no-op, relevance and useful novelty jointly, stable IDs through repeated reads, and responsive updates on actual evidence |
| Direct edits can personalize onboarding conservatively | Actual prior-game manual edit vs. untouched default, accepted recommendation, and Undo | Accurate evidence and converted values, applicable analogues only, and zero fabricated durable preferences |
| A grounded comparison advances understanding | Existing unilateral rationale vs. concise relevant concept or value comparison | Factual correctness and no unsolicited proposal in automated checks, plus independent decision and navigation in a human study |
| Editable review can sustain user steering | Repeat prompting vs. direct correction, explicit apply, and targeted recovery | Correct staged values and provenance, fewer repeated actions, and no unrelated committed changes |

The upstream studies support these hypotheses as design choices. Their observed outcomes apply to their own participants, tasks, and models. Claims about GamePlan should name the matching evaluation and sample.
