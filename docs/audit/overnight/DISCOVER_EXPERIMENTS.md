# Discover experiments

The existing policy groups simulated context events, follow-ups, goal matches and onboarding checks, then selects a small stable set. It preserves familiar menu access, and cards can point back to the exact catalogue control. Its order can favor items the player has already changed or discussed, and its aligned state teaches mainly when other queues are empty. Set **AGENT_DISCOVERY_POLICY=utility** to exercise the alternative on the same [discovery service](../../../backend/app/domain/recommender.py).

Utility considers goal or preference score, whether the setting was recently linked or already changed, cross-game evidence, source quality, a recent context event and the review cost of a suggested edit. It filters pending draft items and shows at most one candidate per setting. Explicit protection blocks edits. With an active objective or repeated manual evidence, the system can reserve space for one read-only control with a relevant catalogue trade-off. Existing availability, dismissal and held-card behavior apply. Fresh evidence can offer a refresh while the current cards stay stable.

| Scenario | First-audit policy | Overnight utility policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Useful choices after simulated frame drops when VSync and Reflex are already pending | Passes. Other eligible context edits appear. | Initially failed because passive onboarding crowded out all context edits. Giving a recent event explicit weight restored eligible alternatives. |
| Player wants to see enemies in dark areas, protects every video setting, and already tried contrast and Dynamic Shadows | No qualifying unfamiliar inspection in the scored fixture. | After real edits, early ranking surfaced FSR. The revised ranking instead offers Global Shadow Quality, comparing it to the encountered Dynamic Shadows. No video edit is staged or applied. |
| Fresh CS2 after manual VALORANT sensitivity, voice and ping changes | Passes five provenance, scope and restraint checks. | Passes the same five checks. Cross-game education stays ahead of generic onboarding. |

The independent fixture identifies useful setting families before selection. It checks canonical target, incremental advice, protected settings and data provenance. The exposure proxy sees saved changes and recent linked answers. It cannot tell whether the player saw the card, understood it or has years of experience with the setting.

The alternative requires zero additional model calls per event. A lexical overlap between catalogue labels provides a simple way to identify a contrastive counterpart after real interaction. It can miss related controls with unrelated names, and availability and category checks still apply. A model reranker on explicit request remains plausible for complex educational choices. The first-pass utility regression argues for keeping the default for the presentation. Enable utility in a labelled rehearsal of the protected-video inspection. A participant study can measure whether an unfamiliar card helps players locate and explain a control independently after assistance. This follows the feature-awareness measure in [Findlater and McGrenere](../LITERATURE_REVIEW.md), and the focus on usefulness and intervention cost in [Todi et al.](../LITERATURE_REVIEW.md).
